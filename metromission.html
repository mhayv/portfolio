CONTEXT

Negative behavior on public transport can often be addressed early in life by building better habits from a young age.

This project explores how a gamified learning experience can encourage users to develop positive habits and foster greater empathy and consideration for others in shared public spaces.

THE CHALLENGE / BRIEF

WHAT ARE WE SOLVING?

The client, Auckland Transport, wanted us to help people feel and be safer when using Public Transport in Auckland.

SURVEY

The first thing our team did was send out surveys to gain insight on the general publics view on what 'positive etiquette' looks like, and how they usually react to 'negative behavior'

(Survey sent out to gain general insights)

The key insights we gained from this survey was :

  • A large number of people think youth need education on public transport etiquette
  • People do not speak up when they witness disruptive behaviour
  • Most people thought education on etiquette should be taught

INSPIRATION

We did case studies on existing campaigns other public transport services did to promote positive etiquette on public transport, and found Singapore's Gracious Commuting Campaign from 2014

(Singapore Gracious Commuting Campaign - 2014)

The team was very inspired with the bright use of colors and fun mascots that embody different aspects of positive etiquette, and began ideating ways we could design a game experience to meet our target audience where they are
